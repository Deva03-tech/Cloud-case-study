## Open threats to validity

- **The simulator failed its frozen validation** (results/validation.json: p95 −92 %, CPU-seconds −55 % vs a real Docker s2 replay). Isolated-query calibration plus a FIFO k = c queue ignores CPU contention between concurrent queries, so simulated latencies under load are optimistic (lower bounds). Relative cost rankings rest on billed capacity, which the failure affects less, but no claim about meeting the p95 target under load is supported until the model gains a contention term and passes re-validation.
- **No real serverless measurement.** No Neon `.env` arrived, so serverless behaviour (15 s resume, scale-up doubling every 5 s, scale-down halving after 60 s, 0.25 vCPU-eq minimum) is modelled from AWS documentation and assumptions, not measured. The scaling constants are not documented by AWS. Neon RTT was not measured, so RTT = 0 everywhere.
- **Validation is simulator vs real Docker provisioned (s2), not vs a real serverless replay.** It checks the queueing and service-time model only; it cannot test resume, scaling or billing. Billed compute-seconds match by construction, so compute-seconds were validated as consumed CPU-seconds. The replay was 10 min (planned 45 min on Neon).
- **CPU quotas, not pinned cores.** Docker sizes use `--cpus` quotas on a hybrid i5-13450HX (6 P + 4 E cores); WSL2 vCPUs do not map to fixed core types.
- **Free-plan 5-min fixed suspend.** Neon Free suspends after a fixed 5 min. Timeouts other than 300 s (S-tuned candidates 60/120/600/1800 s) correspond to paid plans (Neon Scale: 1 min to always on; Aurora: 300 s minimum, so 60/120 s are not available on Aurora).
- **Single machine, single run of calibration.** One laptop and one calibration pass (3 cold + 5 warm per query and size); no confirmatory repeat.
- **Capacity equivalence** (1 vCPU = 1 Neon CU = 2 Aurora ACU) and linear per-vCPU RDS pricing are modelling choices. The RDS price comes from a third-party mirror of the AWS price list.
- **S-tuned is selected in-sample** on the same 10 seeds it is evaluated on (optimistic). **P-sched resizes instantly** on a known schedule; real RDS resizes take minutes. Redset arrivals are mapped to TPC-H queries at random, and P-sched on Redset uses an oracle hourly schedule.
- **Small scale factor** (TPC-H SF 0.1, 8 queries) and short simulated horizons (3 h for the synthetic sweep, 24 h for Redset).
- **Contention model (sim v2) is fitted on s2 only** (s1/s4 cut) and applied to all capacities; its linear form max(1, a·n/c) has systematic residuals (over-predicts at n ≤ 4, under-predicts at n = 8; results/calib_contention.json), and the slowdown is fixed at query start rather than re-shared as load changes.
- **Docker /dev/shm (64 MB default) caused DiskFull errors** under concurrency (results/calib_contention.json) and most likely the unrecorded queries in validation v1; failed queries are excluded from real latencies, biasing real p95 low under heavy load. The container config was kept identical across v1 and v2.
- **Redset P-mean latency is backlog drain, not service time:** hourly ρ < 1 but 21 minutes with ρ > 1 (results/redset_overload_check.json).
- **DiskFull errors from Docker's 64 MB /dev/shm affected both real replays (v1: 96 unrecorded queries; v2: 61 logged DiskFull) and the concurrency calibration (25 DiskFull at n = 4 and 8).** Failed queries are excluded from the real p95, so real tail latency under load is under-stated. Fix: `docker run --shm-size=1g`.
- **The s2 contention fit is applied to all sizes** (0.25 to 4 vCPU-eq) in sim v2; s1 and s4 were never measured under concurrency.
- **Non-reproducible closed-loop seeding:** the contention calibration seeded query choice with Python's per-process randomised hash(), so the exact query sequence cannot be replayed.
- **Latency in sim v1 and sim v2 is not validated** (p95 error −88.5 % and +34.6 % on trace 9191); only sim v2 CPU-seconds passed (−1.4 %). The cost headlines rest on that check, but serverless billed compute also depends on the modelled scaling controller, which no replay tested.
